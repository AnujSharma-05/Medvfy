MedVerify flow map

How a dose travels from the son's phone to the father's pill box and back, what the software does at each step, and the decisions needed before building the caretaker and patient apps.

Does yesterday affect today's result?

The detection score does not carry over. Each frame is compared only with the calibration photos you saved with E (empty) and S (loaded). Nothing learns from earlier frames or days. What does carry over is the compartment's last state, on purpose, so a restart doesn't announce the same removal again.

ThingCarries over?What that means for testing
Detection scoreNoAlways "this frame vs. your calibration photos". A new object layout only needs a new E + S.
ThresholdsNoFixed numbers in settings.json. They change only when we tune them from your test data.
Compartment stateYesSaved in vision_state.json. If A ended a test "emptied", the next run starts from "emptied". Pressing S resets every compartment to "loaded".
Dose historyYesKept for the son's reports. It never feeds into detection.

With many object permutations, the state carry-over is the part that would make testing messy. The proposal below adds a trial mode: every trial starts from a clean state, is labelled with what you actually did, and never sends alerts to the son.

1 · What runs where

Everything runs on the laptop at home. The son reaches it through a public link that the laptop opens outwards (a tunnel), so the home router needs no setup. The camera only ever talks to the server on the laptop itself.

2 · A day, from each person's side

Read each column top to bottom (or bottom to top, where the father acts first). The first three columns are the normal path. The next two are what goes wrong, and the last is the weekly refill.

3 · The life of one dose

This is the server's rule book. It acts only on confirmed camera states, so a passing hand or a blurry frame cannot close a dose.

4 · Teaching the algorithm with your webcam

"Teaching" here means measuring. You perform labelled trials, the recorder saves what the camera measured, and an evaluator replays those measurements under different thresholds. It reports how many removals each setting would miss and how many false alarms it would raise. We pick the setting from that report. Nothing changes on its own while you test.

Flaws this design has to answer

FlawWhat would go wrongProposed answer
Laptop on the internetAnyone with the link could post fake "compartment emptied" events, or change the schedule.Camera endpoints answer only to the laptop itself. Son's pages need a login. The tunnel provides HTTPS.
Laptop asleep or offNo reminders, and a dead laptop can't tell the son it's dead.Never-sleep power plan. An outside watchdog (e.g. a free healthchecks.io ping every minute) alerts the son when pings stop.
Stop button erases the doseThe father silencing the beep would mark the dose cancelled.Split it: father gets "Stop sound" (dose stays open); only the son can cancel.
Recalibration needs the keyboardAfter a refill, someone must press S in the camera window at home.Server sends "recalibrate" to the camera; the son or father triggers it from their app.
Shareable link changesWith a free quick tunnel, the son's bookmark breaks after every restart.Use a fixed link (decision 1). Alerts go through ntfy, which doesn't depend on the link.
Time zonesIf the son lives in another time zone, "08:00" is ambiguous.Dose times are always the father's local time, and the app labels them that way.
Test runs pollute real historyDozens of test removals would alert the son and fill his reports.Trial mode: separate history, no alerts to the son.

Decisions I need from you

Answer by number. Each one has my recommendation, so "go with your picks" is a valid answer.

  1. Shareable link(a) Cloudflare quick tunnel: free, no account, new random link at every start. (b) ngrok: free account, one fixed link. (c) Cloudflare with your own domain: fixed and branded, needs a domain.Pick (b) for real use; (a) is fine for a one-time demo.
  2. Son's loginOne caretaker account with a password set on first start, or a secret link with no password?Password. A secret link can be forwarded by mistake.
  3. Where the father sees his screen(a) The laptop's own screen next to the box. (b) An old phone or tablet on home Wi-Fi. (c) No screen, light and beep only.Pick (a) now; (b) later works with the same page.
  4. Father's buttonsOnly "Stop sound", or also "I took it"? The camera decides either way. A self-report that disagrees with the camera would be shown to the son as a conflict.Only "Stop sound" to start. Fewer buttons, fewer mistakes.
  5. Language on the father's screenEnglish, Hindi, or both side by side?Both, big text, plus the compartment letter shown huge.
  6. Photos for the son(a) None. (b) A crop of the box at each event. (c) Live view.Pick (b), opt-in. It shows the box only, never the room.
  7. What the son can do remotelyEdit the schedule, start a reminder now, cancel a dose, and mark "confirmed by phone call".All four. The last one keeps his reports honest when he checks in by phone.
  8. RefillsWho refills the box, and how often? Daily, weekly, or by the son on visits?This decides whether recalibration must be one tap for the father or can be a step for the son.
  9. Trial mode for testingSeparate test history, clean state per trial, no alerts to the son.Yes, and build it first, so tonight's webcam session produces usable data.
